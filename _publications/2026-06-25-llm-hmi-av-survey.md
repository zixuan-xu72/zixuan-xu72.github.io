## Abstract

Autonomous vehicles should do more than drive: they should understand people, explain decisions, and communicate in ways humans can understand and act on. This survey examines how Large Language Models (LLMs) are becoming a new interaction layer for human-machine interaction in autonomous vehicles.

Reviewing 68 studies from 2022 to 2025, we organize this emerging field as a closed interaction loop: **MODEL - REASON - ACT**. MODEL captures how LLMs interpret human states, behaviors, and intentions; REASON describes how they integrate context, safety constraints, and human needs to decide what and how to communicate; and ACT explains how they translate vehicle states and human requests into understandable, executable interactions.

## Key Contributions

- Provides a comprehensive survey of LLM-enabled human-machine interaction in autonomous vehicles
- Reviews 68 studies published from 2022 to 2025
- Proposes the MODEL - REASON - ACT framework for organizing LLM roles in automotive HMI
- Discusses trust calibration, over-reliance, safety validation, and real-world deployment challenges
